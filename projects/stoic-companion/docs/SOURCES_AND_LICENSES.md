# Sources, editions and rights

The authoritative version-controlled registry is `corpus/sources.json`. It stores each source's author, translation, edition/publication information, language, URL, SHA-256, approval status and rights rationale. Approval is independent of ingestion-job state. `python -m app.ingestion status` reports both.

1. [Meditations, Gutenberg 2680](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2680): Meric Casaubon translation. The source's own edition notes identify Casaubon and discuss the 1634/1635 numbering. Only the twelve books, not the modern introduction/glossary/appendix, are indexed. The catalog labels the ebook public domain in the USA. Exact later print-edition publisher information is not asserted because the transcription does not supply a complete title page.
2. [Epictetus, Gutenberg 10661](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/10661): George Long translation (translator died 1879), with both named Discourses selections and the Encheiridion. The catalog identifies the translator and USA public-domain status. It has no reliable original chapter-number map; references use printed headings. Exact print-edition year/publisher is not supplied by the transcription and is not invented.
3. [Seneca, Wikisource](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius): Richard M. Gummere's Loeb translation, William Heinemann / G. P. Putnam. Volumes appeared 1917–1925; the catalog title page identifies Gummere. Only [Letter 1](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_1), [Letter 13](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_13), and [Letter 63](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Moral_letters_to_Lucilius/Letter_63) are approved here. These pre-1931 publications are public domain in the US in 2026. Gummere died in 1969; life+70 jurisdictions may protect the translation through 2039. Original web provenance is retained. Wikimedia markup/metadata can carry CC BY-SA terms even when the underlying translation is public domain; attribution links and the original HTML notices are preserved.

Blocked candidates:

- [Higginson Enchiridion, Gutenberg 45109](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/45109): the file includes a 1948 Salomon introduction and 1955 editorial changes. Gutenberg lists USA public-domain status, but this implementation avoids mixing those layers without separate review. Long is used instead.
- [On the Shortness of Life](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/On_the_shortness_of_life): the supplied page identifies a 1932 translation and claims nonrenewal in the US, with translator death in 1959. Translator identification and renewal evidence were not independently established here. It is blocked, not downloaded or quoted. No modern Seneca translation has been substituted.

The compact `corpus/snapshots.zip` contains the **original unmodified bytes** of approved source downloads, including notices. Its member checksums match the manifest. This small archive (rather than duplicated raw directories) ensures offline reproducibility and avoids depending on dynamic Wikisource HTML hashes. Ingestion extracts only the named approved member and verifies it; raw and separately normalized files are kept in ignored local data. Missing snapshots fall back to their documented source URL, at one request/second; changed content fails checksum validation and needs explicit review. Never blindly update checksums.

Sources were inspected on November 12, 2026. Public-domain status here is US-focused and is not a worldwide license assertion. Before distributing elsewhere, verify the translation and all editorial layers against the destination's law. Preserve Gutenberg's source license and trademark conditions; the raw archive retains the full notices even though indexed text omits boilerplate.
